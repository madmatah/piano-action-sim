# Dynamics prototype: one note of Hirschkorn's action in motion

**This prototype is throwaway.** It is plain Python 3 with numpy, written to
settle questions, not to be ported. It is not the engine, and none of the
engine's rules about how code is built (no allocation in a step, `libm` for
every transcendental, bit-reproducibility across machines) apply to it. It is
kept in the repository only so that the decisions it supports have a source
anyone can rerun.

## What it settles

Three hypotheses of `docs/FOUNDATIONS.md` §4, each by the test §4 gives it:

- **§4.1, the time step.** One fixed step, chosen by a step-convergence study:
  the same scenarios at decreasing fixed steps, until the finger's force and
  the observables (the let-off instant, the hammer's speed at let-off, drop,
  checking) stop changing by more than their resolution.
- **§4.2, one dynamic solver.** Integrator (B) is the hypothesis: compliant
  contacts treated implicitly at velocity level, axis friction as bounded
  unknowns, a fixed step. Integrator (A), explicit penalty, is what it is
  compared with. The tests are step convergence, Hirschkorn's blows, Thorin's
  key force under position drive, and the trade's protocols run on both.
- **§4.3, the felt law.** The law here is Hirschkorn's cubic with its damping
  and the clamp (`model.py`, A3). The test compares felt laws on his piano and
  forte blows.

What this directory holds is what those tests run on: the one-note model, its
two integrators, the two drives, and the level-0 consistency checks; and
`study.py`, which runs the tests of §4.1 and §4.2 and writes what they find,
with the recommendation, to `RESULTS.md`.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `model.py` | The model: coordinates, mass matrix, velocity terms, the thirteen contacts (signed gaps, normals, contact points, lever arms), the contact laws, axis-friction bounds, springs, gravity, energies, script 10's closure, and the frictionless static equilibrium. Its docstring lists every assumption, A1 to A10. |
| `integrators.py` | (A) explicit penalty, semi-implicit Euler; (B) linearly implicit at velocity level. Both use a fixed step, and both solve set-valued axis friction as a box-constrained problem by projected Gauss-Seidel, under a cap, reporting the iteration count. Also the drives: free, constant torques, force at the key front, position of the key. |
| `scenarios.py` | The initial state, the position and force drives, a runner that prints the initial state's thirteen contacts, a time series, let-off, strike, checking, the finger's force and the solver diagnostics; and four studies, `slope-study`, `letoff-geometry`, `chatter-study` and `capstan-study`. |
| `checks.py` | The level-0 consistency checks. |
| `study.py` | The studies that settle FOUNDATIONS §4.1 and §4.2: step convergence of four scenarios under four integrators (A and B as built, and the same two with contact friction made a set), the descent study behind the scenarios' durations, the stiffest linearised modes along each scenario, and operation counts per step. It writes `RESULTS.md`. |
| `RESULTS.md` | The studies' tables and recommendation, generated by `study.py`; not edited by hand. |

## How to run

Python 3 and numpy, nothing else. Each file finds the record
(`data/params/hirschkorn-boston-gp178-note52.json`) and
`tools/derivations/10-hirschkorn-assembled-at-rest.py` by its own path, so it
runs from any directory. No bytecode is written.

```bash
python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/checks.py        # about 25 s; exit status 1 if a check fails

python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/scenarios.py position --integrator B --dt 2e-6
python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/scenarios.py forte --integrator A --dt 5e-6 --t-end 0.3
python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/scenarios.py piano --integrator B --dt 5e-6 --t-end 0.5
python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/scenarios.py rest --integrator B --dt 1e-5 --t-end 0.05
python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/scenarios.py slope-study      # about 2 min
python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/scenarios.py letoff-geometry
python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/scenarios.py chatter-study
python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/scenarios.py capstan-study
python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/study.py        # writes RESULTS.md; about 9 min on 20 cores
```

A step costs about 90 µs in either integrator, so 0.25 s of action at 2 µs takes
about 12 s.

## The model in brief

The model has five coordinates, Hirschkorn's (Eq. 2.1, Figure 2.3): key, wippen
and hammer about their ground pins, and jack and repetition lever relative to
the wippen about their pins on it. Every value comes from the record, read
through script 10, so the two cannot disagree on geometry. Inertia is Table A.1's,
an internal parameter. The thirteen contacts are Table A.8's, between the
circles, lines and hybrids of Tables A.2 to A.7. The normal law is Eqs. 2.4 and
2.5 with the clamp. Contact friction at contacts 1, 4, 7 and 10 follows Eq. 2.10
with its bracket, its threshold speed assumed at 1e-5 m/s. Axis friction is
Table A.9's, set-valued. The springs are as §A.4 gives them, the jack's sign
chosen. The finger acts at Pk2, vertically. `model.py` states each assumption
and its source.

## The level-0 checks

Every check is a **consistency check** (FOUNDATIONS §5.1): it shows that the
model agrees with itself, with mathematics or with script 10. It catches
coding errors, and a wrong model passes it. None of them is a validation.

1. The mass matrix against central finite differences of the kinetic energy,
   computed from the bodies' point motion; against script 10's matrix; against
   FOUNDATIONS §3.6's closed forms.
2. Every contact's gradient, over all five coordinates, against central finite
   differences of its gap at random poses; the same identity read as speeds,
   against the motion of the two bodies' material points, with the slip
   Jacobian on the tangent.
3. The hybrid normal, continuous across both ends of both arcs: on the
   primitive, through the assembled contact, and along real motions of the jack
   and the lever.
4. The signed gap, negative through penetrations deeper than the circle's
   radius, for all thirteen contacts.
5. Energy in free flight, with contacts, friction, springs and gravity off:
   bounded, its departure first order in the step, in both integrators.
6. The rest pose equal to script 10's: the same closure, every gap the same to
   its printed precision.
7. A body held by axis friction stays exactly still, bit for bit, under a torque
   below its bound, in both integrators, alone and in the assembled action.

These rest on further checks: the velocity terms against Lagrange's equations,
the conservative forces against the potential, the contact laws as printed
(including the static peaks of Eq. 2.10), the jack spring's sign, the friction
solver against an exhaustive enumeration, and both integrators agreeing on a
forte-like blow.

A last group re-derives the model and runs it:

- The record read afresh and the action assembled with code shared with
  neither `model.py` nor script 10 (complex numbers, the hybrid by projection
  on its wedge): every gap, the mass matrix, the wippen block's closed forms
  and every gradient agree with it, at random poses and at states through a
  key stroke.
- At those states each normal points from body B to body A, each contact acts
  midway between its two surfaces, the normal and friction forces are the
  virtual work of forces at that point, and friction never does positive work.
- Gravity points down in the record's frames, each body falling onto its own
  support; the repetition spring lifts the lever towards the knuckle, against
  its stop.
- Axis friction holds every joint at 0.9 of its bound at once, through loaded
  contacts, bit for bit; a joint at 1.05 starts in its net torque's direction,
  the others exactly stuck.
- With contact damping and all friction off, the total energy through contact
  closures stays bounded in (A) and never rises in (B).
- A key driven down in 0.3 s goes through let-off with the knuckle at most
  0.04 mm into the jack and the toe 0.03 mm into the button, and the hammer
  comes to rest on the repetition lever, held by the drop screw.

## Reading its output

Four properties of the model as built, which anyone reading its numbers needs:

- **Script 10's closure is a pose, not a rest state.** The lever overlaps its
  stop by 0.88 mm there, about 16 N that the repetition spring cannot hold. Every
  scenario starts from the frictionless static equilibrium. There the hammer
  stands 48.16 mm from the string, the knuckle rests on the jack alone with the
  lever 0.84 mm below it, and the shank rests on its rail with 0.04 N
  (`scenarios.py rest`).
- **Let-off comes early, and drop after it, with the geometry as printed.** At
  let-off the hammer is 11.4 mm from the string on the forte-like force drive,
  12.7 mm on the piano one, 12.9 mm on the position drive and 13.0 mm on a key
  driven down in 0.3 s. Rigid and quasi-static, the jack's toe meets the
  button with the hammer 13.1 mm from the string (`letoff-geometry`).
  Hirschkorn regulated 1.0 mm. With the key held at the bottom, the hammer
  then rests on the repetition lever, held by the drop screw, 10.4 mm from the
  string (`checks.py`, the slow key stroke): nearer the string than at
  let-off, so the lever lifts the hammer after the jack has left it.
  Hirschkorn regulated the drop to 3.0 mm, below a let-off of 1.0 mm. The
  key's dip at Pk2, from rest to the front-rail punching, is 9.3 mm against
  his 10.0 mm (the same check). The record gives no list of the geometric
  parameters his regulation of the model changed. Checking comes out at 13.5
  to 16.4 mm across the three drives, against his 15.0 mm.
- **Contact friction chatters wherever a contact sticks under load.** Eq. 2.10
  is integrated explicitly in both integrators (`integrators.py` gives the
  measured reason for (B)). Its stable explicit step at a sticking contact is
  set by f_n A (1 + B1) / v_t against the tangential mass: between 1 and 2 ns
  at the back check holding the hammer under 90 N. At microsecond steps the
  friction force flips sign from step to step, and the finger's force flips
  with it. On the position drive's hold it ranges from 20 to 120 N around a
  mean of 52 N at 2 µs, and the checked hammer creeps 0.37 mm towards the
  string between 60 and 160 ms (the runner's hammer-string column). Restarted
  at 1 ns, the back check sticks and the finger's force settles at 60.8 N
  (`chatter-study`). The runner counts these reversals per contact. The
  jack/knuckle and the capstan do the same during the stroke, wherever their
  slip passes through zero. The hammer's speed at the strike still converges
  (2.2643 m/s); the finger's force carries the capstan's chatter. Around 29 ms
  of the position drive the capstan's friction, about 4.9 N, flips sign from
  step to step, and the finger's force jumps by about 2 N between consecutive
  steps at 4, 2 and 1 µs alike, 0.4 N at 0.2 µs, 0.08 N at 0.05 µs (the runner
  reports the largest such jump before the toe meets the button). Averaged
  over 0.1 ms it agrees with the 0.05 µs run within 0.04 N at 2 µs and 0.01 N
  at 1 µs (`capstan-study`). Once a joint slides past its axis-friction bound,
  the jack/knuckle's chatter takes over its motion at 10 µs. The jack at 1.05
  times its bound turns +8.1 to +8.9 µrad in 10 ms, or -1.7 to -1.9 µrad,
  depending on the signs of the torques on the other joints, its friction at
  the knuckle flipping on 475 to 995 steps of 1000; the wippen and the hammer,
  held at half their bounds, move too. At 1 µs the jack turns +0.055 µrad in
  every case and the others stay still (`chatter-study`).
- **The back check holds the hammer by wedging.** On the hammer, its friction
  arm is about five times its normal arm (131 against 26 mm/rad in the hold),
  so any friction coefficient above about 0.2 locks it, and Table A.8 gives
  0.434. The lock keeps the elastic energy of the catch in a stiff law: about
  90 N of normal force through the hold.
