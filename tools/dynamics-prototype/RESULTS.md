# Dynamics prototype: step convergence, stiffest modes, cost per step

Generated by `study.py` (`python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/study.py`): every number in
this file is printed by it, from runs of `model.py` and `integrators.py` used unchanged. It
settles `docs/FOUNDATIONS.md` §4.1 (the time step) and §4.2 (one dynamic solver) on the
one-note model of Hirschkorn's action, and says what can and cannot yet be said about §4.3
(the felt law) and §4.5 (rigid bodies). What the model assumes is in `README.md`. With the
geometry as printed, let-off comes about 13 mm from the string where Hirschkorn regulated 1
mm (`README.md`, "Reading its output"): that moves the scenarios' let-off, drop and
checking, not the way they converge. Every result here is a consistency check in the sense
of FOUNDATIONS §5.1: it says how the model behaves under its integrators, not that the model
is right.

## In short

- **Contact friction decides how small the step must be.** As built, Eq. 2.10 is integrated
  explicitly at contacts 1, 4, 7 and 10 with its threshold speed at 1e-5 m/s, and wherever a
  loaded contact sticks its friction changes sign from one step to the next: 218,419
  reversals in the 485,000 steps of the forte press at 1 µs. Its 1 µs references are then
  not converged: in the forte press checking moves by 0.090 mm between 1 and 0.25 µs, at
  first order, towards the set model's value, and the piano press's hammer speed at the
  string sits on a plateau the chatter sets, which a step of 0.25 µs does not leave (the
  section on contact friction below). A and B hold every observable within resolution of
  those references up to 5 µs at most, in any scenario. With contact friction a set, as the
  axes already are (A-set, B-set), the reversals fall to 73 and the observables converge. An
  implicit Eq. 2.10 with its saturation would stop the chatter too (FOUNDATIONS §3.5); it is
  not built here.
- **The step is 50 µs.** With A-set, every trade observable (let-off, aftertouch, drop,
  checking, the rest regained after release, D and U), the hammer's speeds within 1 %, the
  slow press's key force within 1 g and the piano press's within 0.1 N hold up to 50 µs in
  all four scenarios. A-set stays finite up to 160 µs in every scenario and first diverges
  at 180 µs, in the forte press. The forte press's key force is the one curve that needs a
  finer step to stay within 0.1 N: 2 µs. Its deviation is first order in the step, about 1.2
  g per µs of step before let-off and 4.5 g after it (5 µs suffice before let-off, 2 µs
  after), and it is gathered where the capstan sticks and just after the back check's catch.
  At 50 µs that curve's 1-norm deviation from the reference, Thorin's J, is 0.97 %.
- **The solver is A, explicit, with both frictions as one set.** B is stable at every step
  tried, but its linearly implicit Euler damps every contact mode and loses energy at each
  impact in proportion to the step: the back check catches the hammer 25 µs late per µs of
  step in the forte press, and checking drifts with the step. B-set's largest step within
  resolution, against A-set's: slow press 2 µs against 50 µs; forte press none against 2 µs;
  piano press 2 µs against 200 µs; forte by force 2 µs against 100 µs. Its stability buys
  nothing here: A-set's limit lies above every step its accuracy allows.
- **The stiffest mode met is 1475 Hz**, in the forte press: #11 lever / drop screw 100 %
  (7.7 N). FOUNDATIONS §4.1's starting estimates, recomputed by script 10 at its assumed
  fortissimo forces, are 1174, 2240 and 2268 Hz; the largest forces the scenarios produce
  are tabulated against those assumed below.
- **A note costs about 2431 floating-point operations a step** with A-set, plus 15
  transcendental calls: 49 Mflop per simulated second at 50 µs, 4.3 GFLOP/s if all 88 notes
  moved at once.

## The four integrators

| integrator | contact normal laws | contact friction (contacts 1, 4, 7, 10) | axis friction | code |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| A | explicit: semi-implicit Euler | Eq. 2.10, explicit, v_t = 1e-5 m/s | a set | `integrators.py` |
| B | linearly implicit: one Newton step of backward Euler on their stiffness and damping | Eq. 2.10, explicit | a set | `integrators.py` |
| A-set | as A | a set, solved with the axes | a set | `study.py`, `step_set` |
| B-set | as B | a set, solved with the axes | a set | `study.py`, `step_set` |

A-set and B-set are a variant built for this study, to measure what the owner's choice of
contact-friction law changes; `model.py` and `integrators.py` are not changed. Their contact
friction is Eq. 2.10's limit as v_t goes to zero, which leaves no threshold speed: a contact
that slides carries A f_n against its slip; one that sticks carries any force up to A p f_n,
p the law's peak over A (1.000, 1.686, 1.725, 1.128 at contacts 1, 4, 7, 10). f_n is the
normal force at the start of the step, and which bound applies depends on whether the
contact ended the previous step stuck, hidden state the step carries. Each step solves one
box problem at velocity level: one row per free joint (bound A_i dt) and one per loaded
frictional contact (its slip row, bound mu f_n dt). A projected Gauss-Seidel sweep guesses
which rows stick, and an exact active-set pass, pivoting from that guess, finishes the
solve. Where stuck rows are redundant (a joint and a contact holding the same body) it takes
the split of least utilisation, sum (lambda_i / bound_i)^2, so the split, and the finger
force that depends on it, does not drift from step to step. The checks at the end of this
file test it.

## Observables and the resolution each is judged at

| observable | read as | resolution | why that resolution |
|:---|:---|---:|:---|
| key force, slow press | the force at Pk2, vertical, that drives the key through the profile (the key's own row of each step); its mean in 0.08 mm bins of key travel, going down and coming up, compared bin by bin | 1 g | touchweights are read to the gram (Stanwood: down and up weight "to the nearest gram") |
| D, U (slow press) | the mean of that force over key travel 2 to 6 mm, going down and coming up: this press's counterparts of the down and up weight at its speed, not the trade's protocol (a quasi-static search for the weight that starts the key), which is the quasi-static solver's | 1 g | as above |
| key force, forte and piano presses | the same force in 0.2 ms windows (5 kHz, the rate of Hirschkorn's load cell) over the descent, compared window by window, split at let-off | 0.1 N | the trade has no reading of force in a blow; the measured curve it will be compared with, Thorin's position-driven key force, comes from a sensor specified within ±1 % of its usual 10 N scale (docs/research/A2 §9.2), so a step moving the curve by less cannot change that comparison. The piano press's key force peaks at 5.7 N, within that scale; the forte press's at 86 N, past it, so 0.1 N is stricter there than that sensor |
| let-off | the hammer-to-string distance at the instant the jack leaves the knuckle (contact 7 opens after the toe has met the button), interpolated within the step (FOUNDATIONS §1.3) | 0.08 mm | the thinnest punching paper, the finest regulation quantum the trade uses anywhere in the action (FOUNDATIONS §1.3); the trade specifies hammer-side distances to 1/32" (0.79 mm) at best (docs/research/A3 §2.1), so it is a strict choice |
| aftertouch (the let-off instant) | the key travel at Pk2 from the let-off instant to the bottom (9.65 mm, the key 0.3 mm into the front punching); in the forte by force, whose bottom is not prescribed, the key travel at let-off | 0.08 mm | a punching paper: aftertouch is set through the key dip, with papers |
| hammer at let-off, at the string | the speed of the hammer's striking point towards the string (the rate of contact 12's gap) at the let-off instant and at the instant the gap closes, interpolated | 1 % | the trade has no reading of it; the reproducing piano that records it, the Bösendorfer SE, counts the time over the last 5 mm at 25.6 kHz, a quantum of v²/128 m/s: 0.8 % at 1 m/s, 1.6 % at 2 m/s (docs/research/A5 §7.2) |
| drop (slow press) | the hammer-to-string distance with the key held at the bottom after let-off, the hammer resting on the repetition lever, mean over the last 20 ms of the hold | 0.08 mm | as let-off |
| checking | the hammer-to-string distance with the key held at the bottom after the blow, the hammer caught by the back check, mean over the last 20 ms of the hold | 0.08 mm | as let-off |
| rest after release | the hammer-to-string distance at the end of the run, the key released and the action back at rest (the largest joint speed at the end of the set model's 1 µs runs is 2.5e-18 rad/s; as built, where contact friction chatters, 3.8e-03 rad/s; the runs start from the frictionless equilibrium, the hammer 48.16 mm from the string) | 0.08 mm | as let-off |

A deviation is measured from the reference run of the same model (below); a value outside
its resolution is marked `*` in the tables. The key force while the key is still is an
interval (the key's own axis friction) and is not judged.

## Step convergence

Each scenario runs at fixed steps of 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200 and 500 µs under each
integrator. The reference of a model is the 1 µs run of its more accurate integrator, the
one whose 2 µs run departs less from its own 1 µs run, in resolutions; the other
integrator's 1 µs run is the cross-check. As built and with the set are two models, each
judged against its own reference.

The cross-check fails in the forte press only, for each model, and on the key force. As
built, the 1 µs runs are not converged: contact friction chatters (the section after the
tables). With the set, B-set departs from A-set at 1 µs on the key force after let-off only:
B's numerical damping delays the back check's catch, and the force at the finger rises by
tens of newtons within a millisecond there. Below 1 µs, B-set closes on A-set's reference
(the second table after the convergence tables).

approaches the set model's as that speed falls. Whether the slow press's differences are the
law's or the chatter's, the sweep does not say. The piano blow is where Hirschkorn's own
model fails (FOUNDATIONS §4.3), so the friction law belongs in that test alongside the felt
law, integrated so that it converges.

## Why B needs a smaller step than A

B solves the contact laws by one Newton step of backward Euler. That is stable at any step,
but backward Euler damps an undamped mode of frequency omega by 1/sqrt(1 + (omega dt)^2) per
step, so an impact loses energy in proportion to the step. In the forte press the hammer
leaves the string slower and returns later, and the back check catches it 25 µs late per µs
of step:

## Recommendation

### §4.1, the time step

- **One fixed step of 50 µs, with A-set.** Every trade observable in all four scenarios is
  within its resolution there: let-off, aftertouch, drop, checking, the rest regained after
  release, D and U, and the hammer's speed at let-off and at the string within 1 %. So are
  the slow press's key force (1 g) and the piano press's (0.1 N). It is under a third of the
  step at which A-set first diverges (180 µs), and below the least linear limit met (69 µs).
  That limit is set by the contact damping and falls as a blow gets harder, so the margin is
  the forte press's, not the step's: a harder blow, which the printed geometry jams (the 25
  ms descent), is to be run once the model is regulated.
- **The one curve outside its resolution at 50 µs** is the forte press's key force under
  position drive: 57 g before let-off and 224 g after it, against 10 g, with J = 0.97 % over
  the descent. Read within 0.1 N it needs 2 µs: a run of the same solver at a finer fixed
  step, done offline when the bench compares a forte key force with a measured one (1,215
  Mflop per simulated second for the note). Read within 1 % of its own peak, 86 N, it holds
  to 20 µs.
- **No sub-steps on the real-time path.** No observable but that curve asks for a finer step
  anywhere. That curve's error is gathered at events: before let-off at 18.7 ms, where the
  capstan sticks (18.60 ms), 57 g at 50 µs against at most 16 g elsewhere before let-off;
  after let-off at 33.1 ms, 0.7 ms after the back check's catch, 224 g against at most 66 g
  elsewhere. Sub-steps at those events might keep it within 0.1 N at a larger step than 2
  µs; that is not tested here.
- The keyboard drives its keys by force (FOUNDATIONS §7.6), as the forte by force does, and
  that scenario holds every observable up to 100 µs with A-set.

### §4.2, one dynamic solver

- **Integrator A, explicit, with the axis and the contact friction in one box problem at
  velocity level (A-set).** Its accuracy sets the step; B's stability is never needed below
  A's limit, and B's numerical damping costs it a far smaller step for the same observables:
  B-set holds 2 µs (slow press), none (forte press), 2 µs (piano press), 2 µs (forte by
  force), where A-set holds 50 µs, 2 µs, 200 µs, 100 µs.
- That revises the hypothesis as FOUNDATIONS §4.2 states it, compliant contacts treated
  implicitly: measured, the contact laws are better explicit than under backward Euler, the
  implicit step tried here; an implicit step without its numerical damping is not tested
  (what remains open, below). The quasi-static solver is then not the dynamic step's static
  limit: it stays a separate solve on the same contacts and the same friction sets, inertia
  dropped, as §4.2 describes it.
- The box problem has 4 to 8 rows. One PGS sweep and an exact active-set pass solve almost
  every step; for the real-time path a fixed budget of a sweep and a bounded number of
  pivots keeps the cost per step bounded (FOUNDATIONS §4.4).

### The decision the owner owes: contact friction

- As built, Eq. 2.10 is integrated explicitly, and at the steps tried it either converges
  slowly, at first order (checking within a punching paper needs a step of 0.5 to 0.6 µs),
  or sits on a plateau its chatter sets (the piano press's hammer at the string, barely
  moving from 1 to 0.25 µs, +2.8 % from the set model's). That says nothing against the law
  itself: integrated implicitly with its saturation, which this prototype does not build, a
  velocity-only law sets no step limit (FOUNDATIONS §3.5). What counts against it is its
  threshold speed, which has no physical value and would stay in every sensitivity, as §3.5
  argues for the axes; where the law is resolved, that speed moves the piano press's strike
  speed (the section on contact friction).
- Its limit as the threshold speed goes to zero, a set with static coefficient A p and
  kinetic A, converges at 50 µs and holds a body it sticks exactly still. Against the
  as-built model at its smallest step it differs beyond resolution on the slow press's
  hammer at let-off (1.42 resolutions, +1.4 %) and the piano press's hammer at the string
  (2.85 resolutions, +2.8 %), and on the key force in the slow press, forte press and piano
  press; at those steps the as-built model has not converged, so these differences are not
  yet the law's, and the piano press's is the chatter's.
- Recommended: contact friction as a set, like the axes, for that reason, and because it is
  the variant measured here. The choice is the owner's.

### What remains open

- **§4.3, the felt law.** Its test is each law against Hirschkorn's measured forte and piano
  blows, which exist only as figures in his thesis (Figs. 3.9 to 3.13 and 4.1 to 4.15) and
  are not digitized: no law has been compared. What this study does say: the cubic with its
  damping and the clamp runs stably with A-set up to 160 µs; its tangent stiffness sets the
  stiffest modes and its damping the least explicit limit, so a stiffer law, a more damped
  one, or one with steep unloading branches, moves the step: this study reruns with each
  law. Without hysteresis, the hammer caught by the back check keeps the catch's elastic
  energy: in the forte press the back check holds it with 61 N through the hold, and the
  finger pushes 42 N to keep the key down, 5 N of it on the front punching. A
  rate-independent law with loading and unloading branches would release most of that. The
  piano press uses Table A.8's forte-calibrated damping factors; Hirschkorn's piano
  recalibration is not printed. As built, the contact friction's chatter moves the piano
  press's hammer speed at the string by +2.8 % at every step tried, so the friction law has
  to be integrated so that it converges before that test reads the piano blow.
- **§4.5, rigid bodies.** Not tested: the model has no degree of freedom between head and
  shank, and the record gives no shank stiffness. Thorin's hammer has its first mode at 39
  Hz (FOUNDATIONS §4.5); the forte press's stroke from rest to let-off lasts 20 ms here, so
  (f tau)^-1 = 1.3: the mode is not fast against the stroke, and a flexible shank should
  show in the key force under position drive. The added coordinate brings a 39 Hz mode, far
  below the stiffest contact mode met (1475 Hz), but it also splits the hammer's mass, so
  the string and the knuckle see less of it: the stiffest modes and the explicit limit have
  to be recomputed with it, which this study does by rerunning. The system grows from 5 x 5
  to 6 x 6.
- **The regulation.** With the geometry as printed let-off comes about 13 mm from the
  string. Regulated to Hirschkorn's 1 mm, the hammer leaves the jack about 1 mm from the
  string, so let-off, the strike and the lever's stop on the drop screw come much closer
  together in time. Rerun this study once the model is regulated, with a blow harder than
  the forte press: the least explicit limit falls as the force at the jack/knuckle rises.
- **A second-order explicit step** (the velocity-Verlet form of A) could relax the forte key
  force's step; it is not tested here. Nor is an implicit step without numerical damping
  (theta = 1/2), which would matter only where A's limit falls below the step accuracy
  allows: a stiffer law, or a harder blow, since the least limit is the contact damping's.
- **One note.** Note 52 only: heavier bass hammers and lighter treble parts move the modes.
- **The profiles.** The presses are smoothsteps, zero speed at both ends, not measured key
  motions; the forte by force is shaped from Hirschkorn's description.

## Checks of the set variant

- With every contact-friction bound zero, A-set reproduces integrator A on the model without
  contact friction: 17000 steps of a 60 ms forte press at 5 µs, positions identical, speeds
  within 8.9e-16 rad/s, finger force within 1.8e-15 N.
- The box solver against an exhaustive enumeration of active sets, on 300 random problems of
  its shape (four free speeds, six rows, rank four): generalized speeds within 2.8e-13, at
  most 1 PGS sweep before the exact pass.
- Contact friction never does positive work: over every step of every A-set and B-set run
  that stays finite, the largest ratio of a sliding contact's friction power to its bound is
  -1.000000 (-1 when sliding against its bound).
- A body held by friction stays still: at the end of the hold, the largest joint speed in
  the A-set and B-set references of the forte, piano and forte-by-force scenarios is exactly
  zero.

## Reproducing

`python3 -I tools/dynamics-prototype/study.py` reruns every run and rewrites this file; here
the runs took 527 s on 20 worker processes. `--save FILE` also keeps the raw results, and
`--load FILE` rewrites this file from them without running. The runs use numpy and the
platform's math functions, so another machine may differ in the last digits (`README.md`).
