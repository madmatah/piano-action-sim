# Vocabulary: open questions

The French–English mapping lives in one place,
`docs/research/A3-nomenclature-and-regulation.md` §6. This file holds only what is
still open in it, each question written so that it can be answered without opening
anything else: the options, what each means, and what the answer changes. An answer
goes into A3 §6, and its question leaves this file.

This file is deliberately bilingual: the French terms are the subject matter. No
source is cited for a term; the trade's vocabulary belongs to the trade.

## The action

**1. « Cuillère de chevalet ».** A numbered sectional plate of a grand action lists a
« cuillère de chevalet » among the wippen's parts, between the « bouton de réglage de
l'engagement du bâton d'échappement » and the « fourche de support du levier de
répétition ». The grand's damper under-lever has a spoon of its own, the « cuillère
de bascule », which rests on the lift rail; on an upright the spoon is on the damper
lever. Is there a « cuillère de chevalet » on a grand, and if so, what is it and
what does it touch? Or is the name used loosely for the under-lever's spoon?
*What the answer changes:* whether the wippen gains a part and a contact in the
model.

**2. « Vis de chute » and « vis de réglage de la hauteur du levier de
répétition ».** The plate numbers them separately. In the makers' regulation manuals
they are two screws with two jobs. One, on the wippen, sets the repetition lever's
height against the top of the jack, which removes lost motion between jack and
knuckle. The other, the drop screw above the lever, on the hammer flange or the
hammer rail, stops the lever after let-off and so sets the drop. Do the two French
names map onto them one to one, « vis de réglage de la hauteur du levier » for the
first and « vis de chute » for the second? Or, in your usage, does either name also
cover the other screw?
*What the answer changes:* the French labels of two regulation settings, the jack
to lever height and the drop.

**3. « Pilote d'échappement », « poupée » and « bouton d'échappement ».** The let-off
button is « bouton d'échappement ». The glossary also lists « pilote d'échappement »
and « poupée » for it, and contradicts itself on the first: one entry treats
« pilote d'échappement » as a plain synonym; another reserves it for one form of the
part, the long wooden dowel screwed onto a threaded point under the hammer rail in
Schwander-type actions, as against a button turning on a screw, as on Steinway.
Which is right:
(a) synonyms, whatever the form of the part;
(b) « pilote » only for the long wooden dowel;
(c) another distinction?
And do you say « poupée » for the let-off button?
*What the answer changes:* whether the interface shows one name for the let-off
button whatever its form, or names the dowel form apart.

**4. « Barre d'échappement » and « barre des boutons d'échappement ».** The glossary
maps both to one identifier, the let-off rail: the rail, mounted under the hammer
rail, that carries the let-off buttons. One rail with two names, or two different
rails? If two, what is the other one?
*What the answer changes:* one identifier or two.

**5. « Ressort auxiliaire (de chevalet) ».** The glossary maps this one French term
to two identifiers: one glossed "auxiliary / wippen support spring", the other
"wippen assist / helper spring". Both glosses describe a spring that helps lift the
wippen, so that the key needs less lead for the same down weight. One part under
two English names, or two different springs? If two, what tells them apart, and
what is each called in French?
*What the answer changes:* one identifier or two, and whether the wippen stage
models one kind of support spring or two.

## Dampers and pedals

**6. « Taquet d'étouffoir ».** A figure of a Schwander-type grand action labels a
« taquet d'étouffoir » in the damper group, beside the sostenuto tab (« bec de
sostenuto ») and the damper wire. Which part is it, and what does it do?
*What the answer changes:* whether the damper model has a part it does not yet
name.

**7. « Sommier des bascules ».** On a grand, « sommier » is the pinblock, never an
action rail. A damper chapter writes « le sommier des bascules » for the frame the
damper under-levers pivot on, which the figure beside it labels « châssis des
étouffoirs ». Is « sommier des bascules » an accepted second sense of « sommier », or
loose usage?
*What the answer changes:* whether the interface ever says « sommier » for anything
but the pinblock.

**8. « Barre de forte », « barre de levée des étouffoirs », « plateau de levée ».** The
rail that lifts every damper under-lever when the damper pedal is pressed is the
« barre de forte »: that is settled. The glossary records « barre de levée des
étouffoirs » in one place as the name of the whole lifting assembly, and in another
maps « plateau / barre de levée » to the same identifier as the rail. Which is
right:
(a) « barre de levée des étouffoirs » is another name for the « barre de forte »;
(b) it names the whole assembly, the rail with its pivots, springs and felts;
(c) something else?
And is « plateau de levée » said at all?
*What the answer changes:* whether the assembly needs an identifier of its own.

**9. « Prisonnier » and « pitman ».** Under the keybed, a wooden dowel rises from the
damper pedal's lever to lift the damper lift rail. The glossary gives it two
identifiers: « prisonnier » (tourillon de transmission de forte) as one, « pitman »,
the English trade's word, as another, and glosses « pitman » elsewhere as « goupille
de sostenuto ». One part or two? Is « pitman » said in French? And is « goupille de
sostenuto » the sostenuto pedal's own dowel, a different part?
*What the answer changes:* one identifier or two, and whether the sostenuto's
transmission gets a part of its own.

## The key frame

**10. Dag blocks.** The hardwood blocks glued to the keybed behind the key frame's
back rail, which hold the frame down while letting it slide sideways with the una
corda. The glossary has « patins arrière », marked uncertain; a sectional plate
lists a « cale arrière de châssis de clavier », which may or may not be the same
part. What do you call them?
*What the answer changes:* the French label of the dag blocks.

## Names in pairs

**11. Parts and operations listed under two French names.** Each pair below is
mapped to a single identifier. For each, which applies: both names in use for the
same thing (and then, which one should the interface show?), one of the two wrong,
or two different things?
- « noix d'étouffoir » and « bloc d'étouffoir » / « bouton d'étouffoir »: the block
  clamping the damper wire in the under-lever, whose depth sets each damper's lift
  timing.
- « tige d'étouffoir » and « fil d'étouffoir »: the damper wire.
- « galerie des étouffoirs » and « barre de guidage des étouffoirs »: the rail whose
  bushings guide the damper wires.
- « bec de tonale » (« bec de sostenuto ») and « languette de sostenuto »: the tab on
  the under-lever that the sostenuto rod catches.
- « barre de tonale » (« barre de sostenuto ») and « tige de sostenuto » / « lame de
  sostenuto »: the sostenuto rod and its blade.
- « tige de pédale » and « tringle de pédale »: the rod from the pedal up to its
  lever.
- « rouleau » and « noix de manche »: the knuckle. The glossary also says that
  « noix » on a grand is the damper block, and the hammer butt only on an upright.
- « dressage » and « hauteur de touche »: one table gives « hauteur de touche » for
  the height of the keys, in millimetres, and « dressage » for levelling them to one
  another; the identifier table maps « dressage (niveau) des touches » to the
  height.
- « défauchage », « fauchage » and « chauffage des manches »: one table pairs
  « défauchage / fauchage » for traveling the hammers and burning the shanks
  together; another gives « défauchage » for traveling and « fauchage / chauffage
  des manches » for burning. Which word names which operation?

*What the answer changes:* the word the French interface shows; where two things
turn out to be different, a second identifier.

## The interface

**12. The una corda pedal.** On a grand it shifts the key frame and the action
sideways, so that the hammers strike fewer strings, or a different part of their
felt. Which name do you use: « pédale de transposition », « pédale de décalage »,
« petite pédale », « pédale douce », « pédale una corda »? Is any of them wrong for a
grand?
*What the answer changes:* the pedal's French label; its identifier stays the
same.

**13. The two « chevalet » in the French interface.** « Chevalet » names both the
wippen (in the action, the assembly of wooden parts: heel, repetition lever, jack
and the rest) and the bridge (the wooden bar linking the strings to the
soundboard). Should the interface always qualify them, « chevalet de mécanique » and
« chevalet de table », or say plain « chevalet » in the action's panels and qualify
only where the bridge appears too?
*What the answer changes:* the wording of every panel and fault message that names
the wippen.

**14. The German column.** The glossary gives « Tastenfront » for both the key front
(« fronton ») and the front rail (« barre d'enfoncement »), and « Hammerleiste » for
both the hammer rail (« barre des marteaux ») and, as « Hammerleiste (Polster) », the
hammer rest rail (« barre de repos des marteaux »). If you know the German trade
terms, which belongs where? If not, should the German column stay empty for these
rows?
*What the answer changes:* the German column only, no identifier.
